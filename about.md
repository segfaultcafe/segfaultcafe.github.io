Greetings! I'm Alex Buschinelli - or João Buschinelli in Brazil.

I'm a senior software engineer who enjoys building systems that keep working when the happy path stops being happy. Most of my professional work lives around Go, asynchronous workflows, messaging, distributed services, and the unglamorous but essential details that make integrations reliable: idempotency, retries, state machines, observability, and good tests.

At PicPay, I have worked on payment and partner integrations, high-throughput queue-based pipelines, gRPC service orchestration, shared authentication architecture, and developer tooling that makes end-to-end testing less dependent on external systems. I like the point where architecture becomes practical: fewer timeouts, clearer failure modes, and software that other teams can confidently build on.

My path into engineering started in physics. I studied cosmology at McGill University, worked with large simulation datasets, and learned to be comfortable with problems whose answer is not obvious at first glance. That habit followed me from scientific Python and C into backend engineering, security, and systems programming.

Outside work, I keep wandering into ambitious technical rabbit holes. I build edge/cloud home-security software with Go, Rust, PostgreSQL, SQLite, and physical camera hardware. I explore rendering and explicit resource management in a C++23 and Vulkan game engine. And I am building [Flecha](https://github.com/alexbsec/flecha), a statically typed systems programming language and LLVM-based toolchain documented throughout this blog.

Security is still part of how I think. I have reported vulnerabilities in open-source software, including remote-code-execution and access-control issues, and I carry that adversarial curiosity into API design, authentication, native code, and distributed systems.

This blog is where I write down what I learn while building all of it. Expect compiler internals, systems design, security, graphics, and the occasional deep dive that began with a simple question and got wonderfully out of hand.
